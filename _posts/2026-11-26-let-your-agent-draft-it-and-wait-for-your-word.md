**Let Your Agent Draft It — and Wait for Your Word**

Have you ever pressed Send, then spotted the mistake a second too late — a wrong name, a wrong date, words that sounded harsher than you meant?

There is a calmer way to send messages, and it uses your assistant like a careful secretary.

Give it the job, and give it one clear instruction with it: draft the message, then wait. Nothing goes out until you have read it and said yes.

You can change the draft as much as you like, in plain words — "add the lunch place", "take out that line". The draft just sits there, patient, until you decide. If you don't like it at all, nothing was sent, and no harm was done.

Try it with a message you actually need this week:
1. Say who it is for, and what it is about — a birthday, a thank-you, a family lunch.
2. Add these words: "Show me the draft. Do not send it yet."
3. Read it slowly. Ask for one change.
4. Only when it sounds like you, send it yourself — or tell your assistant, "Send it now."
